Easter lily · Lilium longiflorum Checked for cats
Each source
ASPCA

Supports: classification (toxic to cats); effects including kidney failure, and that death is possible; kidney failure within 48–72 hours; very small exposures, including pollen; cats as the only species known to be affected.

Refers to: Easter lily · cats

Independence: not verified.

Pet Poison Helpline

Supports: classification; all plant parts; eating any part of a lily from the Lilium genus; water from a vase holding lilies.

Refers to: lilies (Lilium genus) · cats

Independence: counts as one source together with MSD Veterinary Manual.

Cats Protection

Supports: classification; pollen picked up by rubbing against the flower and licking the fur; advice not to have lilies in the home or garden.

Refers to: lilies · cats

Independence: not verified.

MSD Veterinary Manual

Supports: kidney failure 2–4 days after ingestion; peace lily and calla lily listed as 'imposter' lilies.

Refers to: lilies · cats

Independence: MSD Veterinary Manual and Pet Poison Helpline count as one source here, because a Pet Poison Helpline toxicologist wrote the MSD chapter.

International Cat Care

Supports: vase water, for lilies; 'pollen-free' lilies are still unsafe.

Refers to: lilies · cats

Independence: not verified.

Where sources agree
  • Classification — ASPCA, Pet Poison Helpline, Cats Protection
  • Kidney failure — ASPCA, MSD Veterinary Manual
  • Vase water, for lilies — Pet Poison Helpline, International Cat Care
